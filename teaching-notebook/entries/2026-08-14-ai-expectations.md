
# Using AI in My Classroom: Expectations and Accountability

**Date:** August 14, 2026  
**Course:** BMGT 240 — Building Sustainable Futures  
**Institution:** University of Maryland Global Campus  
**Format:** Course announcement and accompanying student guide  
**Author:** Professor Argüelles Ortiz

## Context

At the beginning of the course, I shared my expectations for students who choose to use generative AI in their coursework.

Drawing from my professional experience using AI, I wanted to communicate that AI adoption and independent judgment are not mutually exclusive. Students are encouraged to experiment with AI, but remain responsible for the quality, accuracy, and originality of their submissions.

## Original Classroom Announcement

### I use AI every day at work. If you plan to use it in this course, here are my Expectations

*Originally posted August 14, 2026.*

Hello all,

AI is already part of how many of us work, and I encourage you to use it in this course as well. But using AI effectively means **challenging, reviewing, and refining its output not simply accepting it.**

Your work should ultimately reflect **your** experiences, perspectives, and judgment. I've attached a short guide outlining how I expect you to approach AI, including reviewing its output, validating sources, and making sure your work remains your own.

Please take a few minutes to read it a you begin submitting your first assignments. Once you've read it, if you like it, dislike it, or simply want to discuss it, please feel free to reach out.

Best regards,

Professor Argüelles Ortiz

## Accompanying Teaching Resource

[Introduction to Using AI for BMGT 240](../materials/intro-to-using-ai-bmgt240.pdf)

The guide outlines three expectations:

1. **Review, Review, Review:** Evaluate AI-generated work against assignment requirements, your own experiences, and previous feedback.
2. **Check Your Sources:** Confirm that cited sources exist, support the claims being made, and are relevant and authoritative.
3. **Tell Me How You Used AI:** Encourage transparent acknowledgment of AI tools used in coursework.

The emphasis is on responsible use rather than prohibition.

## Instructional Approach

This communication establishes several expectations:

- AI may support brainstorming, research, organization, and critical reflection.
- Students should challenge and refine AI-generated outputs.
- Submitted work should reflect each student's own perspectives and judgment.
- References suggested by AI should be independently verified.
- Students are invited to disclose their use of AI.
